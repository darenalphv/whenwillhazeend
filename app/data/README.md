# `app/data/` — datasets and provenance

Everything in this directory is bundled. The app works with no network and no API keys.

**Read this first:**

> **The fire-cluster geometries in `fires/` are synthesised. They are not fire detections.**
> Every point, size, date and fire-radiative-power value in `app/data/fires/fires_*.json` was
> generated by us. No fire happened at that coordinate on that date. Only two things about
> those files are tied to published statistics: each year's `area_ha` total reconciles exactly
> to that year's `burned_area_mha_kalimantan` in `seasons.json`, and the split between provinces
> interpolates between the two KLHK per-province recaps we were able to locate. If you want real
> per-fire records, use [NASA FIRMS](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/) active-fire data or
> the MCD64A1 burned-area product.

A machine-readable version of the table below is in `provenance.json` (loaded by
`load_provenance()`, served at `GET /api/provenance`, and rendered in the site's Methods
section). The two must stay in sync.

## Status vocabulary

| status | means |
|---|---|
| `measured` | taken directly from a published measurement or official statistic |
| `estimated` | derived from published figures under an assumption we state |
| `synthesised` | generated by us to be consistent with published aggregates; corresponds to no observation |
| `modelled` | a parameter of the model, not a property of the world |

## Season window rule

`start_date` / `peak_date` / `end_date` in `seasons.json` are derived from ERA5 daily
precipitation averaged over three southern-Kalimantan peat-belt points (Palangka Raya,
Banjarmasin, and Kapuas/Sebangau):

- **start** — first day on or after 1 May with 30-day antecedent rainfall < 120 mm
  (relaxed to < 180 mm for years where the primary threshold is never met: 1998, 2020, 2021, 2025);
- **peak** — day of minimum 30-day antecedent rainfall;
- **end** — first day more than 7 days after the peak with 21-day rainfall > 180 mm.

The rule reproduces the documented episodes well — 2015 gives 5 Jul → 13 Nov (131 days),
2019 gives 12 Jul → 15 Oct (95 days), 1997 gives 30 May → 10 Dec (194 days) — but it is
still a **rainfall** rule. A hazard model calibrated on these durations is partly
calibrated on its own input; that circularity is real and is not fixed by the fact that
the dates look right.

**2010 and 2022 have no window at all** under either threshold. `duration_days` is `0`
and the dates are `null`. Both were strong La Niña years; the 30-day rainfall never fell
low enough. That is the model's floor, and it is a real signal, not missing data.

## Known disagreements between sources

These are not resolved, and the site should not pretend they are.

- **Burned area.** KLHK puts 2019 at 1.65 Mha; a Sentinel-2 study (Gaveau, Descals & Salim **2026**, *PLOS ONE* 21:e0331831) puts it
  at 2.64 Mha, and other satellite estimates run to 3.8 Mha. The site now shows burned area
  as a range (`burned_area_mha_*_range`) wherever the sources disagree; the difference is
  larger than most of the effects the model is trying to resolve.
- **Peatland extent.** BBSDLP 2011 gives Kalimantan 6.0 Mha; KLHK's current figure is
  4.5 Mha. The fuel-exhaustion date depends directly on which you believe.
- **1997/98.** Tacconi 2003 revised the Indonesia-wide total from 9.7 Mha to 11.7 Mha, and
  the split between the two calendar years is not well defined.
- **Peat's share.** Peat was 33% of the 2015 burned *area* but around 50% of Equatorial Asia's
  fire *carbon* in every large year, because peat burns downward rather than outward.
