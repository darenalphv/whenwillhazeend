    Methods, caveats & provenance

    This section is not collapsed, and it is not an appendix. Everything above is a model output. Here is what the model does, what it cannot do, and where its numbers come from.

    How the season number is produced

    A discrete-time daily survival model. For each day after today the model computes a termination hazard from recent rainfall, the Keetch–Byram Drought Index, the ONI index and the progress of the northeast monsoon, then Monte-Carlos thousands of trajectories. The headline is the median of the simulated end dates; the interval is the 10th to 90th percentile.

    How the exhaustion year is produced

    A stock-and-flow model over five fuel pools, with ENSO evolving as a three-state Markov chain. "Exhausted" means burnable stock has fallen below 5% of its 2025 value. It is arithmetic on assumptions, carried out to a horizon far beyond anything anyone can forecast. It is here to show a direction, not a date.

    How the picture at the top is drawn

    The haze over the answer is not decoration. Its density is set from the season model's Keetch–Byram Drought Index and its probability that the season is still burning two weeks from now. A wet season leaves the sky clear; a bad one hides the far treeline and turns the sun into an orange disc you can look at directly, which is what the real thing does. Where the browser can run it, a 3D model of the island's smoke fades in over that sky once the answer has loaded; it never replaces the answer.

    How the odds are counted

    Each percentage in the odds section is the share of the season model's simulated trajectories in which the event happened. There is no market behind it, nobody stakes anything, and the numbers move only when the model's inputs do.

    Who built it

    This site is built and run by ALPHV Technologies, an AI-native systems integrator in Kuala Lumpur. The model, its caveats and its error rate are published here in full either way.

    What this is not

    It is not an air-quality service. If you need to know whether it is safe to go outside today, use your national agency's measured readings, not a model on a website with a joke for a domain name.

    The haze is not a natural disaster with a season attached. It is drained peat, cleared land and a market for both. The worst seasons on record are associated with tens of thousands of excess deaths across Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore, and with billions of dollars of economic loss. Where published studies disagree on those figures, the page shows the disagreement rather than the most dramatic number. They are not a punchline.
